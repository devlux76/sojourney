# Crater Lake National Park: reference imagery and map data

Compiled 2026-10-02. Research only: nothing here ships. Everything downloaded sits under `tools/.cache/reference/crater-lake/` (gitignored), organised as `ortho/`, `layers/`, `places/`, `seasons/`, `underwater/`, `species/`, each with a `SOURCES.csv` (filename, source URL, page URL, author/agency, date, licence text as stated where checked, notes). By instruction, all material is treated as look-reference; licences were not audited (the Commons and iNaturalist rows keep the licence the source states per file).

Everything listed below was opened or queried during this session unless marked "not verified". Park bounds used: 42.76-43.10 N, 122.30-121.98 W; the game square is 42.858-43.020 N, 122.256-122.036 W.

Local total: about 3.2 GB on disk, 2,319 files (ortho 20, layers 1,105 incl. unzipped coverages, places 1,036, seasons 55, underwater 101, species 2). Network download was about 2.9 GB of that (the canopy-height grid was fetched as ~2.2 GB compressed and re-packed to a 1.1 GB GeoTIFF).

## 3. Map layers and how each can drive placement

Vegetation map (`layers/vegetation_vmi/crla_vegmap.gpkg`, layer `CRLA_VegMap_Final`, EPSG:26910, columns `Primary_MapUnit`, `PrimaryAssoc1`, `PrimaryAssoc1_pct`, `SecondAssoc1`, `SecondAssoc1_pct`, `Hectares`, `Recent_Burn`). Area by map unit (hectares): Shasta Red Fir Forest 23,833; Mountain Hemlock Forest 16,397; Lodgepole Pine Forest 11,605; Lake 5,333; Montane Conifer Forest 4,680; Subalpine Fell-field 2,794; Ponderosa Pine Woodland 1,904; Subalpine Fir Forest 1,476; Cliff, Scree & Rock Vegetation 1,201; Mountain Hemlock - Lodgepole Pine complex 925; Mountain Hemlock - Lodgepole - Whitebark Pine Forest 639; Montane Grassland 592; Manzanita shrubland 436; Subalpine Fir - Lodgepole Riparian Woodland 324; Engelmann Spruce Swamp Woodland 314; Whitebark Pine Woodland 299; Pumice/Sand 239; Montane Wet Meadow & Marsh 201; Western Alder Wet Shrubland 181; Antelope Bitterbrush Shrubland 152; Pavement-Development 111; and small units (cherry-oceanspray shrubland, willow, aspen, bog blueberry, cottonwood, subalpine wet meadow, pond). Use: choose tree species and density per polygon, mark meadows, wet meadows, bogs and swamp woodland, pumice/sand flats and rock. The association name gives understorey (e.g. "/ Pinemat Manzanita", "/ Long-stolon Sedge"). `CRLA_VegPoints_Plots` gives ground-cover percentages (Bedrock, Large_Rocks, Small_Rocks, Sand, Litter, Wood, Water, Bare) at 600 points. `tPhotos` lists plot photos by filename and bearing but the photo files were not in the geodatabase package; where they are, I did not find.

Canopy height (`layers/lidar_dogami2010_nps/canopyheight_dm_uint16.tif`, 0.9144 m, EPSG:26910; stored value / 10 is the height, probably in feet). Tree crowns can be picked as local maxima to get tree positions and heights; gaps give meadow and rock edges; buildings and structures show as flat-topped blobs (Rim Village buildings visible). Vegetation survey is 2010 lidar vs 2018 map vs 2022 NAIP, so recent fire or blowdown will differ.

Hydrography (`layers/nhd/`): flowlines (layer 6, named streams include Union, Crater, Red Blanket, Varmint, Bybee, Annie, Castle, Copeland, National, Cascade, Sun, Sand, Munson Creek; Rogue River), waterbodies (layer 12) and points (layer 0). Use for creek and river courses and ponds. The NPS vegetation map's riparian, wet meadow and swamp units add the wet ground beside them. Whether a given flowline carries water in late summer is not stated in what I pulled.

NPS facilities (`layers/nps_facilities/`): POIs (trailheads, campgrounds, picnic areas, viewpoints, restrooms, parking, waterfalls, peaks), buildings, roads, trails, parking lots. Use for roads and paths, trailheads, the two frontcountry campgrounds (Mazama 42.8668, -122.1624 and Lost Creek 42.8797, -122.0387), five backcountry camps (Bybee Creek, Lightning Springs, Dutton, Grouse Hill, Red Cone Springs) and 11 picnic areas. The vegetation package also carries its own `Roads` and `Trails` layers (173 and 287 features).

Geology (`layers/geology_sim2832/sim2832_geology.gpkg`, layers `<quad>_poly`, `_lines`, `_labels`; unit code in `PTYPE`; plus the pamphlet for names and descriptions). Use for rock colour and texture: pumice-fall deposits (cp), ignimbrite units (cf, cu), lava flows, talus (t), alluvium (al), glacial deposits (g), and lake-floor units in the `cl-floor` quad. Unit names for about 84 codes were pulled from the pamphlet text automatically and written only to a scratch file, not to the cache; re-extract if needed. Positions come from a converted NAD27 State Plane grid and are approximate.

Snow persistence (`seasons/s2_*`): compare the 2022-06-21 scene with 2022-09-04 to see where snow lingers into early summer (rim, north-facing walls and the high ground, per the scene I viewed); the March and February scenes show the whole park snow-covered with forest openings and conifer stands standing out.

Orthoimagery (`ortho/`): background check for everything above, and the source for exact road, trail, parking and building footprints at 0.3 m.

Lake floor (`underwater/`): bathymetry for terrain; backscatter and moss model for substrate (moss band and detrital-mat areas) on the floor.

## 4. Gaps

- No named coordinates were found for Llao Bay, Chaski Bay, Eagle Cove, Fumarole Bay, Skell Channel, Governors Bay, Merriam Cone, Old Man of the Lake, Sphagnum Bog, Boundary Springs spring (trailhead only), Wheeler Creek, Lost Creek (stream) or the Cleetwood boat dock in the GNIS or NPS layers I pulled. DDS-72 perspective views (https://pubs.usgs.gov/dds/dds-72/site/persp.htm) show Chaski Bay, Llao Point, Merriam Cone, Wizard Island platform and Phantom Ship/Danger Bay; I did not extract coordinates from them.
- Photos: Wineglass 0, Llao Bay 0, Chaski Bay 0, Eagle Cove 0, Sphagnum Bog 0, Lost Creek campground 0; Redcloud Cliff, Dutton Cliff, The Palisades, Skell Head, Crater Peak, Boundary Springs and Pacific Crest Trail have only 1-2 Commons files each (the USGS photos help for several of these). Underwater images: Commons search found only a few; no ROV or moss-bed stills were downloaded.
- Plot photos from the vegetation inventory (6,102 records) are the best ground-level set, tied to plots with coordinates and bearings. The files were not in the geodatabase zip and I did not locate where NPS hosts them.
- No street-level imagery (Mapillary / Street View) was catalogued with real endpoints.
- No official brochure map file; no trail-by-trail descriptions.
- NPS gallery images, NPGallery, Crater Lake Institute archive and LoC records were not downloaded.
- Seasonal coverage: Sentinel-2 only; no sunrise/sunset, storm, smoke or night-sky reference beyond a few webcam and Commons files (webcam gallery covers smoke, ice, pollen, cloud inversions).
- The NAIP 18 km mosaics have a dark strip on the west side (a no-data gap along part of the left edge); not investigated.
- The geology polygon sampling can pick the wrong unit where quads overlap (checked case: Wizard Island summit returned "ring-vent-phase ignimbrite", which is almost certainly wrong); I did not include geology units in the per-place table for that reason.

## 5. Needs a human decision

- Licences were not audited by instruction. If any of this later becomes shippable, the NAIP, Sentinel-2, USGS, NHD, GNIS and NPS data are US federal or open-data sources (statement not checked here); Commons files and iNaturalist photos carry per-file licences in the CSVs, several are SA or NC.
- Canopy height units (feet vs metres) are an inference from value distribution, not read from the metadata; confirm before using heights.
- Whether to download more of the lidar: the 1 GB highest-hit hillshade, the full 253 GB point cloud, or the newer OR_SouthEast_D22 tiles (extent over the park unverified).
- No logins or paid data were needed. Mapillary and the NPS Data API would need a token or key.
- The auto-assigned place folders in `places/` should be reviewed by eye before relying on them.
