# Crater Lake Fauna and Ground Cover Research

**Purpose:** Realism reference for 3D pocket vacation game (Babylon.js). Elev. ~1,883 m (lake) to ~2,400 m (rim); Wizard Island ~2,113 m. Data sourced from NPS, USFS, OregonFlora, Audubon, Cornell Lab.

## Ground Cover by Zone

### Shoreline (talus, lava rock, water's edge)
- **Kinnikinnick** (*Arctostaphylos uva-ursi*): dwarf spreading shrub, whitish-green leaves tinged red. Year-round ground cover; white flowers spring, red berries autumn.
- **Sedges** (various): dense mats, greenish-brown.
- **Rock lichen, wolf lichen** on talus boulders: grey-green, slow-growing.
- **Mosses** on wet talus edges: vivid green in spring, fading to brown-grey by late summer.

### Wizard Island cinder slopes (4,800 yr old; sparse colonization)
- **Scattered lodgepole & whitebark pine saplings** (100+ year-old largest), whitish bark.
- **Low manzanita seedlings**, **pussypaws** (pink-white flowers June-July), **Davidson's penstemon** (red tubes on grey-green stems).
- **Pumice desert flats**: nearly bare grey-white pumice with sparse herbs, grasses (lichen establishing slowly).
- **Density:** very sparse; large bare scree patches visual.

### Forest floor (hemlock, fir, pine)
- **Huckleberries** (primary ground cover):
  - *Vaccinium membranaceum* (thin-leaved): 30-60 cm tall, soft bright green summer → red-orange autumn; black berries ripen Aug-Sep, 1-1.5 cm diam.
  - *Vaccinium scoparium* (grouse whortleberry): 10-30 cm, tiny leaves, red autumn; small dark berries Aug.
- **Kinnikinnick** (evergreen): pale green, red berries winter forage for birds.
- **Beargrass** (*Xerophyllum tenax*): 30-60 cm rosettes of wiry grass-like leaves; white flower clusters May-Aug (blooms best wet springs). Dense in some clearings, sparse in shade.
- **Fallen logs**: critical microhabitat; nurse seedlings, mushrooms, pika shelter.
- **Mosses, liverworts**: bright green wet season, grey-brown dry; thicken duff.

### Meadows (Castle Crest, rim flats, wet seeps)
- **Anderson's Lupine** (*Lupinus andersonii*): 30-60 cm, purple-blue flowers, July-Aug.
- **Indian Paintbrush** (*Castilleja* spp.): 15-30 cm, bright red-orange bracts, July-Aug peak bloom.
- **Fleeceflower** (*Aconogonon davisiae*): bright green → brilliant red mid-summer → strawberry-brown, July-Sep.
- **Cascade Aster** (*Eucephalus ledophyllus*): cream/yellow, late summer.
- **Sedges, grasses**: dense mats, olive-green, adapted to thin soils (waxy leaves, hair-covered).
- **Density:** 70-90% cover in peak bloom meadows; gaps of bare soil/rock.

### Alpine/subalpine pumice flats (above 2,100 m)
- **Minimal cover:** lichen, moss colonies (slow colonization of barren pumice).
- **Scattered dwarf shrubs:** kinnikinnick, pussypaws, small lupine patches.
- **Wind-scoured, sparse vegetation pattern** gamelike (visual relief).

## Edible/Forageable Plants

**NPS foraging status:** Crater Lake's Superintendent's Compendium permits **hand-picking edible fruits/berries for personal consumption** (per Grand Teton/Denali model); no bush rakes/commercial harvest.

- **Huckleberries** (*Vaccinium* spp.): Aug-Sep ripen; nutritionally rich (wildlife staple, historically gathered by tribes). Purple-black, sweet, slightly tart.
- **Beargrass bulbs** (historical use, rare): not widely foraged now.

**Mushrooms:** presence implies (wet forest soils, mossy logs), but species-specific toxicity unknown; assume toxic unless ID'd by expert (game narrative: "avoid").

## Fauna

### Mammals

**Visible to visitor:**
- **Golden-mantled ground squirrel**: chestnut-golden head/neck with white stripes, grizzled back, pale belly; ~20 cm body. Often at campsites begging for food. Chatters, chips, clicks. Active day, esp. mid-morning.
- **Pika** (*Ochotona*): rabbit-sized (~20 cm), grey-brown, round ears. Rocky talus slopes, Wizard Island cinders. Distinctive "eep" or "eeep" alarm call (high pitch, carries far); shy, summer-autumn active, never hibernates. **Most iconic alpine sound.**
- **Douglas squirrel (chickaree)**: 25 cm, rusty-orange belly, bushy tail. **Noisiest forest animal:** loud "chitter-chitter" rattle and "chickareeeeee" scream territorial; quoo-quoo-quoo contact calls. Constantly vocal, dawn/dusk loud.
- **Chipmunks** (2 species): smaller, striped. Less vocal than Douglas squirrel; chip alarm calls.

**Uncommon (5-month activity window Nov-Mar mostly in den):**
- **Black bear** (~41 individuals): ~200 lbs, cinnamon to black; males range 320-365 sq mi, females 27 sq mi. Bear season late spring-early fall foraging (huckleberries). Sightings rare (low ~5 mo activity).
- **Elk, mule/black-tailed deer**: larger herds historically; deer most frequent visitor sighting (especially rim/caldera meadows before winter). Snort alarm, rustle leaf litter.
- **Pine marten, bobcat, coyote, red fox, striped skunk**: mesocarnivores hunting pika/squirrels in high elevations; nocturnal mostly, silent.

### Birds

**Common/audible:**
- **Clark's nutcracker**: large grey corvid, wingbeats audible in thin air. **Long grating kraak call** ("shraaaaa"), far-carrying; travels small flocks calling back-and-forth. Obsessively caches seeds whitebark pine (visual: hopping on cones). High elevation, late May-Aug abundant.
- **Steller's jay**: crested, deep blue-black upper, grey belly. **Loud "shook-shook-shook" rattle year-round**, plus mimics, guttural growls. Bold, investigates campsites. Canopy foraging, tree-hopping.
- **Common raven**: large black corvid. **Deep, croaking "cronk"** (audible >1 mile), pair duets, high-altitude acrobatics. Soar ridge thermals.
- **Sooty grouse**: subalpine conifer males display spring from high tree perches (100 ft+). **Low owl-like hooting** (6 deep "hoots," air-blown-jug timbre), carries through forest, eerie/atmospheric. Early spring drumming.
- **Dark-eyed junco**: small grey sparrow with white belly, dark hood. **Even musical trill** (7-23 notes, ~2 sec), plus quiet warbles; sharp "kew" aggression call, rapid chip-notes when foraging. Very common, often in flocks, rustling ground.
- **Hermit thrush**: brown-rufous back, spotted breast, orange-red tail. **Haunting, whistle-based song** (oh-holy-holy-ah-purity-purity), echo-like; low "tchup" alarm, mewing kitten-like calls. Leaf-litter foraging, logs/shrubs.
- **Mountain chickadee, Canada jay (grey jay)**: smaller corvids, constant chip-a-dee-dee-dee calls (chickadee) and jay calls. Omnipresent high elevation.
- **Woodpeckers** (hairy, three-toed, black-backed, downy): drumming spring/early summer on snags (tattoo rhythm ~20 hits/sec). Fire-burned forest habitat.

**Nesting/rare:**
- **Bald eagle, peregrine falcon**: caldera cliffs nesting; eagle piercing calls, high soaring. Falcon rapid-fire kek-kek-kek during dive displays.
- **Osprey** (on lake): hunting plunge-dives, chattering alarm calls.
- **American dipper**: only aquatic songbird, fast-flowing streams; bobbing motion, loud trilling call.

**Insects:**
- **Mosquitoes:** June-early July peak (early summer swarming), especially near water/wet seeps; visual hum soundscape element.
- **Butterflies:** daytime meadow pollinators (lupine, paintbrush), visual color flecks (orange, blue, yellow morphs).

### Amphibians/Reptiles

- **Pacific tree frog** (~5 cm): wet meadows, streams. Loud evening croaking chorus (spring-early summer), distinctive "ribbit" call.
- **Cascades frog:** wet meadows >2,400 m.
- **Mazama newt** (endemic): shoreline rocky seeps, dark olive-brown. **Critically endangered by invasive crayfish** (95% shoreline colonized). Nocturnal, silent; larvae neotenic (aquatic lifecycle).
- **Long-toed & Northwestern salamanders:** forest bogs, damp logs.
- **Rough-skinned newt:** rough bumpy skin, orange belly warning coloration.
- **Snakes/lizards:** minimal documentation; assume sparse/silent in shadows (cold elevation).

## Soundscape by Time/Habitat

### Dawn (5:00-7:00 AM) — Forest & Meadow
- **Hermit thrush:** haunting echo-song (first bird), most prominent.
- **Dark-eyed junco, mountain chickadee:** chip-a-dee-dee cascades, rapid trill chorus.
- **Clark's nutcracker:** harsh kraak overhead (tree canopy).
- **Mosquito hum** (June-July peak): low baseline drone near water.

### Mid-day (9:00 AM-3:00 PM) — Mixed
- **Douglas squirrel (chickaree):** continuous territorial rattle, screeching "chickareeee" from high conifers (dominates).
- **Steller's jay:** loud "shook-shook-shook" disputes.
- **Pika:** occasional "eep" from talus slopes (high, sharp, alerts).
- **Bald eagle, raven:** soaring calls (high altitude context).
- **Hermit thrush, junco:** sporadic singing, less intense.
- **Woodpecker drumming** (spring): rhythmic tattoo on snags (20 hits/sec, starts/stops abruptly).

### Evening (6:00-8:00 PM) — Dim light, cooling
- **Hermit thrush:** resurgence of haunting song (second peak).
- **Pacific tree frog:** loud ribbit chorus (spring-early summer, wet areas); female response croaks.
- **Raven, jay:** final calls before roost.
- **Silence gradually** deepens; wind in pines, distant water if near shore.

### Night (9:00 PM+) — Silent except
- **Pacific tree frog croaking** (wet meadows, streams; spring-early summer peak).
- **Occasional mammal rustles** (squirrel, pika, fox—rare audible).
- **Wind through hemlock/fir canopy** (dominant ambient).

### Seasonal overlay:
- **Spring (April-May):** Sooty grouse hooting dominates early morning; frog chorus peaks; migrant songbirds arrive.
- **Summer (June-Aug):** Mosquito hum (June-early July), huckleberry ripening (visual, animal activity increases), chipmunk activity, bird breeding noise.
- **Autumn (Sep-Oct):** Silence deepens; elk bugling; fewer insects; hermit thrush departs.
- **Winter:** Near-silent (animals inactive/hibernating); occasional raven, jay calls; wind dominant.

## Game Design Notes

- **Huckleberries in season (Aug-Sep):** 30-60% visual density on forest floor; ripe berries dark purple-black (pixel color ~#2D1B4E with shine); autumn foliage red-orange (~#C2460E) striking contrast for picking mechanic.
- **Pika "eep" calls:** 1-2 sec intervals, high pierce (3-4 kHz), iconic talus animation trigger.
- **Douglas squirrel "chickareeee":** loudest game sound, territorial, wake-up call; 2-4 notes, ~5-6 kHz.
- **Sooty grouse hooting (spring):** atmospheric tension; low owl-like (100-200 Hz), 6 hoots ~3 sec apart.
- **Beargrass blooms (May-July, peak June):** 5-ft tall white flower clusters visually anchor meadows; sparse in shade, dense in sun-facing slopes (procedural density map).
- **Forest floor fallen log density:** 15-25% coverage in old-growth; pika shelter pathways; mushroom micro-habitats.
- **Talus color:** grey-brown angular rock, lichen patches light grey-green (wolf lichen), moss greener near seeps.
- **Foraging rule:** player hand-picking huckleberries OK (NPS permitted); mushroom avoidance narrative ("don't know if safe").
