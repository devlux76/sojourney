# Flaming Gorge Reservoir — Game Research Guide

> **Reviewer corrections (2026-09-27, supersede the text below):**
> - Precipitation: ~300 mm/yr at the reservoir vs ~1,700 mm at Crater Lake is roughly **5× drier**, not "1.5–2×" or "60 %". Most summer days are dry; rain comes as short (≤1–2 h) July–August afternoon thunderstorms.
> - Rattlesnakes: not the Northern Pacific rattlesnake. The local snakes are the **midget faded rattlesnake** (*Crotalus oreganus concolor*, Green River basin) and the **Great Basin rattlesnake** (*C. o. lutosus*); both are rare and shy.
> - Red Canyon: the overlook sits on the rim roughly **1,400–1,700 ft (430–520 m) above the water**; the "1,631 ft drop / 700 ft wide" figures below are garbled.
> - The rock is Precambrian **Uinta Mountain Group quartzite and sandstone**.
> - Reservoir full pool is ~6,040 ft (1,841 m).

## 1. Place & Terrain

**Geography and Elevation**: Flaming Gorge Reservoir sits at elevation ~6,040 ft (full pool level) in the eastern Uinta Mountains along the Utah–Wyoming border. The Red Canyon rim towers to ~7,400 ft. The 91-mile-long reservoir is framed by red cliffs and has numerous coves, inlets, and small islands. [Ashley National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/flaming-gorge-national-recreation-area-0)

**Geology—The "Flaming" Name**: The landscape is dominated by Precambrian-age red and purple Uinta Mountain Group strata (quartzite, sandstone, conglomerate, and shale, >7 km thick). Below lies older Red Creek Quartzite (~6 km thick). The Uinta Crest Fault runs along the north flank, placing younger sediments against ancient uplift. At sunset, the exposed red sandstone cliffs glow vivid orange-red, earning the "flaming" epithet. [Utah Geological Survey](https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/geosights/geosights-sheep-creek/)

**Sheep Creek Geological Loop & Iconic Play Area**: The 13-mile Sheep Creek Loop showcases ~770 million years of geology with dramatically tilted rock layers and Tower Rock hoodoo formation. Rocky Mountain bighorn sheep inhabit the cliffs. **Red Canyon** (near the Visitor Centre) is the most iconic 2–4 km game area candidate: it provides a 1,631 ft drop from the southern rim into a 700 ft wide, 1,400 ft deep canyon above the reservoir. The red rock walls are the park's signature. [Sheep Creek GeoSights](https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/geosights/geosights-sheep-creek/), [Red Canyon Visitor Centre](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/red-canyon-visitor-center)

**Shorelines & Water**: Flaming Gorge water is blue-green to turquoise due to low suspended sediment and dissolved materials. Summer surface waters stratify into three layers; anaerobic conditions occur at depth. The water is currently lower than historical normals (drought). Shorelines range from sandy beaches (Lucerne Swim Beach, Stateline Cove at ~6,100 ft elevation) to dramatic red-rock cliffs. Maximum depth: >420 ft in places. Winter ice forms by December; summer surface temps become lethal to cold-water kokanee (>75 °F kills them). [USGS Water Data](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/USGS-09234400/), [Lucerne Campground](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/lucerne-campground)

**Digital Elevation Data**: USGS 3DEP provides 1 m and ~10 m (1/3 arc-second) DEMs for the region, free and in NAVD88 datum. Check [USGS Data Catalog](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS:77ae0551-c61e-4979-aedd-d797abdcde0e) and [3DEP FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-can-i-download-3d-elevation-program-3dep-standard-dem-data-bulk).

## 2. Flora by Zone

**Shore (water's edge to ~10 m inland)**:
- Cottonwood (Populus angustifolia/Populus deltoides); 30–60 ft tall; pale yellow-green foliage; spring catkins.
- Willow species (Salix); 15–30 ft; fine, pale green leaves; streamside.
- Density: sparse, gallery-forest patches.

**Sagebrush Flats (10–100 m from shore, elevations 6,000–6,500 ft)**:
- **Wyoming big sagebrush** (Artemisia tridentata wyomingensis); 3–7 ft; silvery-gray, three-lobed leaves; dominant ground texture.
- Density: moderate (5–30 % cover, scattered to dense shrubland).
- Ground cover: native grasses (bluebunch wheatgrass, Indian ricegrass), prickly pear cactus (Opuntia), rabbitbrush (Ericameria).
- Edibles: **serviceberry** (Amelanchier utahensis; 6–12 ft; small purple-black berries June–July; sweet, used for pemmican), **wild onion** (Allium acuminatum; 12 in tall; small pink flowers; bulbs spring–early summer).

**Pinyon-Juniper Slopes (100–500 m, 6,200–6,800 ft)**:
- **Utah juniper** (Juniperus osteosperma); 15–25 ft; scaled foliage, blue-silver cones; dense, twisted wood.
- **Pinyon pine** (Pinus edulis); 20–35 ft; two-needle fascicles, large edible nuts; drops seed cones fall.
- Density: 10–30 %, parkland to woodland.
- Ground cover: grass, yucca, prickly pear.
- Edibles: **pinyon nuts** (Pinus edulis seeds; hard-shell, ~0.5 in; harvest Sept–Oct), **juniper berries** (female cones; blue, resinous; winter–spring), **prickly pear fruit** (Opuntia; red-purple, spiny; late summer), **chokecherry** (Prunus virida; 10–15 ft; small dark berries; July–Aug; tart).

**Ponderosa, Lodgepole, Douglas-Fir Forests (rims, 6,500–8,000 ft)**:
- **Ponderosa pine** (Pinus ponderosa); 80–120 ft; orange-brown bark; sparse understory.
- **Lodgepole pine** (Pinus contorta); 60–80 ft; thin, straight trunks; sometimes dense.
- **Douglas-fir** (Pseudotsuga menziesii); 100–150 ft; soft needles, distinctive 3-pointed bracts on cones.
- Understory: mountain mahogany, Gambel oak, mountain ash.
- Density: 20–60 % cover, varies by elevation and aspect.
- Edibles: **wild raspberries** (occasional; summer), **mushrooms** (fall; puffballs, chanterelles in riparian zones).

**Riparian (creek bottoms, seeps)**:
- Aspen (Populus tremuloides); 40–60 ft; quaking white leaves; golden fall colour.
- Understory: gooseberry (Ribes), currant (Ribes), wild rose (Rosa acicularis), elderberry (Sambucus).
- Edibles: **serviceberry**, **currants** (July–Aug; red/black, small; tart), **wild rose hips** (Sept–Nov; vitamin C), **elderberries** (Aug–Sept; dark purple; cooked only).

## 3. Fauna

**Mammals**:
- **Bighorn sheep** (Ovis canadensis canadensis): ~150–200 lb; rust-brown coat; massive curved horns (rams). Habitat: steep red cliffs, especially Sheep Creek & Red Canyon rims. Sighting: early morning, late afternoon on cliff ledges. Behaviour: alert, explosive bounding. Bleating call. [Sheep Creek Area](https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/geosights/geosights-sheep-creek/)
- **Mule deer** (Odocoileus hemionus): 150–300 lb; gray-brown; large ears; black-tailed. Habitat: sagebrush flats, pinyon-juniper; some swim across reservoir as part of migration (almost a mile). Sighting: dawn/dusk; year-round. Behaviour: cautious, bounds. Snort alarm call. [Ashley NRA Visitor Guide](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r04/ashley/publication/Ashley%20Visitor%20Guide.pdf)
- **Pronghorn** (Antilocapra americana): 90–140 lb; tan with white rump and chest; fastest land mammal in N. America. Habitat: open sagebrush. Sighting: summer (rare at Flaming Gorge proper; more on high plateaus). Behaviour: grouped, bounding. 
- **Elk** (Cervus canadensis): 400–700 lb (cows–bulls); tan–brown; shaggy mane on bulls. Habitat: high forests, winter in sagebrush/pinyon. Sighting: early morning, dusk; most visible late spring–early fall. Bugling (autumn rut, Sept–Oct) is haunting. [Ashley NRA](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r04/ashley/publication/Ashley%20Visitor%20Guide.pdf)
- **Moose** (Alces alces): 900–1,500 lb; dark brown–black; long-legged, dewlap. Habitat: riparian willows, aspen. Sighting: early morning at water sources; rare, solitary. Grunt/bellow call (rut, Sept–Oct).
- **Black bear** (Ursus americanus): 150–400 lb; black coat, brown snout; muscular. Habitat: high forest edges, berry patches. Sighting: early morning, dusk; May–Oct (hibernation). Behaviour: lumbering, will flee humans (mostly). Woofing grunt if surprised. [Ashley NRA](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r04/ashley/publication/Ashley%20Visitor%20Guide.pdf)
- **Mountain lion/cougar** (Puma concolor): 120–220 lb; tawny coat; long tail. Habitat: forested, rocky terrain. Sighting: extremely rare; nocturnal, solitary. Silent predator.
- **Coyote** (Canis latrans): 30–50 lb; gray-tan coat; pointed ears. Habitat: anywhere (sagebrush, forest edge). Sighting: dusk, night, early dawn (most vocal then). Iconic howl/yip-howl; barks. [Ashley NRA](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r04/ashley/publication/Ashley%20Visitor%20Guide.pdf)
- **Marmots & ground squirrels**: yellow-bellied marmot (Marmota flaviventris; 1.5–2.5 lb; high meadows; sharp whistle), chipmunks, Uinta ground squirrel (spotted coat; diurnal; high energy chirps).
- **Cottontail & jackrabbit**: mountain cottontail (1–1.5 lb; gray-brown; clipped tail); black-tailed jackrabbit (larger ears). Sighting: dawn/dusk. Silent flight.
- **River otter** (Lontra canadensis): rare in reservoir proper; 2–5 lb; dark brown; playful. Would animate diving/swimming.

**Birds**:
- **Osprey** (Pandion haliaetus): 24 in wingspan; white underside, dark back. Behaviour: hover over water, plunge-dive feet-first for fish, rise heavily with prey head-forward. Distinctive high-pitched kee-kee-kee whistle. Spring–fall. [Wildlife.Utah.gov](https://wildlife.utah.gov/news/utah-wildlife-news/2141-see-osprey-at-upcoming-viewing-event-at-flaming-gorge.html)
- **Bald eagle** (Haliaeetus leucocephalus): 30 in wingspan; white head, dark body, yellow beak. Habitat: tall cliffs, ponderosa pines. Sighting: winter, nesting spring (increasingly common). Calls: thin squeaks, high-pitched whistles (surprisingly weak).
- **Golden eagle** (Aquila chrysaetos): 30 in wingspan; uniform dark brown. Habitat: cliffs, open terrain. Sighting: soaring, hunting. Silent mostly; occasional kee-kee sounds.
- **Peregrine falcon** (Falco peregrinus): 16 in; slate-blue back, rust breast. Behaviour: fast shallow wingbeats, power dives (200 mph stoops); cliff nesters. Kee-kee-kee alarm. [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/field-guide/bird/peregrine-falcon)
- **Raven** (Corvus corax): 24 in; all-black; deep, raspy croak/caw; acrobatic in flight. Habitat: cliffs, forests; year-round. Very vocal.
- **Pinyon jay** (Gymnorhinus cyanocephalus): 10 in; soft blue-gray; social flocks; loud squeaky calls. Habitat: pinyon-juniper. Fall–winter.
- **Steller's jay** (Cyanocitta stelleri): 11 in; blue crest, dark wings. Habitat: ponderosa/lodgepole. Loud jay! jay! calls.
- **Sage grouse** (Centrocercus urophasianus): 28 in; mottled gray; inflatable air sacs on breast (males). Habitat: big sagebrush flats. Spring lek displays (dawn, loud popping sound from sacs). Rare sighting. [Wyoming Game & Fish](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/)
- **Cliff swallows** (Petrochelidon pyrrhonota): 5 in; blue-black with orange-tan throat. Nesting: thousands under overhangs, dramatic chittering colony sounds.
- **Waterfowl**: Canada geese, mallards, mergansers; loons (call: haunting wails). Summer through fall. [Ashley NRA](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r04/ashley/publication/Ashley%20Visitor%20Guide.pdf)

**Reptiles & Insects**:
- **Lizards**: side-blotched, fence lizards (basking on rocks).
- **Snakes**: garter snakes (common, water-loving); likely northern Pacific rattlesnakes (warning buzz) in lower elevations (not specifically confirmed for Flaming Gorge NRA proper, but present in Utah/Wyoming region).
- **Insects**: dragonflies (odonates; iridescent; over water), mayflies, caddisflies (aquatic), mosquitoes (especially dusk, riparian areas; summer), no-see-ums/gnats (dust, midday).

## 4. Fish

**Lake Trout** (Salvelinus namaycush): Originally stocked for trophy fishery. Now overpopulated (small fish <28 in); anglers encouraged to harvest unlimited <28 in to protect trophy potential and kokanee. Wild record ~51 lb. Depth: 40–200 ft (cold water). Winter under ice. [WGFD Regulations](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/flaming-gorge-management)

**Kokanee Salmon** (Oncorhynchus nerka): Landlocked sockeye; small (~12 in typical), red spawning colour (July–Sept). Declining due to lake-trout predation. Limit: 3/day (Sept 10–Nov 30 closure for shore spawning). Temperature-sensitive; die >75 °F. [WGFD News](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/news-events/anglers-flaming-gorge-encouraged-target-lake-trout-after-reaching-kokanee-limit)

**Rainbow Trout** (Oncorhynchus mykiss): 12–24 in typical; silvery with pink stripe; spring/fall. Moderate population.

**Brown Trout** (Salmo trutta): 12–20 in typical; gold-brown with spots; fall spawning. Lower-density population.

**Smallmouth Bass** (Micropterus dolomieu): 8–15 in; olive-green. Summer warmth tolerated. Shallow bays.

**Burbot** (Lota lota): Invasive bottom-dwelling cod; 12–24 in; mottled gray-brown. Winter active (ice fishing). Increasing. No harvest limit. Anglers encouraged to take to reduce population. [WGFD FAQ](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/Ask-Game-and-Fish/John,-why-is-the-limit-for-lake-trout-in-Flaming-G)

## 5. Weather & Climate

**Compared to Crater Lake**: Flaming Gorge is much drier. Crater Lake gets ~1,700 mm/yr (67 in) with 42 ft of snow. Flaming Gorge gets ~300–400 mm/yr (12–16 in) in Dutch John/Manila area (1.5–2× drier; high desert fringe). [Crater Lake NPS](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/weather.htm)

**Seasonal Temps & Precipitation** (Dutch John station, ~6,811 ft elevation):
- Winter (Dec–Feb): lows ~10–20 °F, highs ~30–40 °F. Snow common; ice on reservoir by Dec.
- Spring (Mar–May): lows 25–40 °F, highs 45–65 °F. Warming; ice-off April–May.
- Summer (Jun–Aug): lows 50–60 °F, highs 75–85 °F. Dry most days; afternoon thunderstorms common (Jul–Aug monsoon influence).
- Fall (Sep–Nov): lows 35–50 °F, highs 55–70 °F. Cooling; fall colour in aspen.
- Annual precip: ~300–400 mm (~60 % of Crater Lake).

**Wind**: Afternoon winds common, 10–25 mph with gusts to 25+ mph. Whitecaps frequent on reservoir. Morning calmer. [NWS Forecast](https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php?zoneid=WYZ029)

**Afternoon Thunderstorms** (July–Aug): Strong, brief (often 1–2 hr). Lightning risk is high (fire danger). Rapid weather changes.

**Sky Darkness**: High desert setting; relatively dark night sky (likely Bortle 4–5 class in remote areas; more light pollution near towns). Clear nights common.

**Hazards**: Summer UV high (thin air, 6,000+ ft elevation); cold water year-round (hypothermia risk).

## 6. Soundscape

**Dawn** (5–7 am, summer):
- Osprey calls (kee-kee-kee, energetic hunting calls).
- Songbirds: warblers, juncos, thrushes (soft, complex songs).
- Elk bugling (autumn only; Sept–Oct; haunting, carries far).
- Light breeze through juniper/pinyon (subtle whisper).

**Midday** (10 am–4 pm):
- Raven croaks/caws (loud, often playful).
- Pinyon jays (squeaky, chattering flocks).
- Cliff swallows (chittering chorus from nesting colonies).
- Wind picks up (afternoon; subtle whoosh through pines, whine across rock faces).
- Insect hum (cicadas, bees, dragonfly wing beats near water).
- Human sounds: distant boat engines (weekends), wind-driven water slap on shore.

**Dusk** (7–9 pm, summer):
- Coyote howl/yip-howl (eerie, iconic).
- Elk cow bugles (softer than rut).
- Crickets chirp.
- Water lapping (wind-driven).
- Occasional loon wail (if waterfowl present).

**Night** (9 pm–5 am):
- Coyote howl (especially clear in cold nights).
- Owl calls (great horned owl hoo-hoo-hoo; barn owl shrieks; if present).
- Nightjar/poorwill (summer; churring calls).
- Wind in canyons (funnelling, eerie resonance).
- Water sounds (gentle lapping, occasional splash from fish/otter).
- Silence—high desert quiet, stars audible (psychological effect).

## 7. Human & Cultural Touches

**John Wesley Powell Expedition (1869)**: Major U.S. geologist and explorer John Wesley Powell led the first well-recorded scientific journey through the Green and Colorado River canyons. He began at Green River, Wyoming, entered the Uinta Mountains in autumn 1868, and spent months navigating the rapids and gorges—including Flaming Gorge. The expedition made ground-truth observations of the geology that gave the gorge its name. Powell later surveyed the area and his insights shaped USGS understanding of river erosion and canyon formation. [USGS Powell Expedition](https://www.usgs.gov/news/powell-expedition-rafting-along-colorado-and-green-rivers-then-and-now)

**Dam & Reservoir (1957–1962)**: Flaming Gorge Dam (arch-concrete, 502 ft tall) was constructed by the Bureau of Reclamation to store water for irrigation and hydropower. Reservoir filled 1962. The dam is visible from certain viewpoints and historically significant; its presence changed the ecosystem and ecology of the Green River dramatically. [USFS](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/flaming-gorge-national-recreation-area-0)

**Campgrounds & Infrastructure**: Red Canyon Campground (high meadow, 6,500+ ft), Canyon Rim Campground (rim views), Lucerne Campground (sandy beach, 140 sites, marina), Sheep Creek Bay, Stateline Cove. Each offers human scale architecture and entry points. Boat ramps, fish-cleaning stations, and visitor centres are functional but minimal.

**Red Canyon Visitor Centre**: Interactive exhibits on geology, wildlife, and history; scenic overlook 1,631 ft above reservoir. Open seasonally. [Red Canyon VC](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/ashley/recreation/red-canyon-visitor-center)

## 8. Hazards & Issues

**Afternoon Wind & Whitecaps**: Predictable afternoon winds (10–25 mph, gusts 25+ mph) create white-capped waves and rough reservoir conditions. Hazard for boating. Animation opportunity: wave/whitecap dynamics.

**Lightning & Fire**: High lightning activity (Jul–Aug thunderstorms). Fire danger peak. Campfire restrictions often in effect mid-summer. Game hazard: sudden weather, fire animation.

**Hypothermia**: Water stays cold year-round (lethal <45 min immersion in winter; ~3 hrs summer). Swimming animation risk.

**Exposed Hazards**: Lower-than-normal water levels (drought) expose rocks, logs, submerged debris offshore. Navigation hazard; animation of revealed rocks at draw-down.

**Rattlesnakes**: Northern Pacific rattlesnake (Crotalus oreganus oreganus) present in Utah/Wyoming; likely in lower-elevation sagebrush (6,000–6,500 ft). Warning buzz if threatened; rarely aggressive. Avoidable with awareness.

**Dust & Erosion**: High desert dust storms possible; red sediment in water during runoff.

**Insects**: Mosquitoes and gnats peak dusk–dawn, riparian areas (especially July–Aug). No-see-ums in dust/high wind.

**Exposure**: High elevation = strong UV, temperature swings, thin air (4–6 % lower O₂ than sea level). Dehydration risk in dry climate.

## Summary for Game Design

**Most Game-Relevant Findings**:
- **Red Canyon near the Visitor Centre is the poster iconic area**: 1,631 ft drop from meadow rim to 700 ft wide, 1,400 ft deep canyon above turquoise-blue reservoir. Red sandstone walls glow at sunset.
- **Water**: Blue-green, clear, cold (summer <75 °F lethal to fish; winter frozen). Dramatic depth variation (420+ ft max) creates visual drama.
- **Afternoon wind**: Predictable 10–25 mph gusts create whitecaps and wave animation; morning calm for fishing.
- **Wildlife**: Osprey diving for fish (spectacular), elk bugling (autumn), coyote howls (night), bighorn sheep on red cliffs (Sheep Creek, Red Canyon). Ravens and pinyon jays add ambient chatter.
- **Soundscape**: Osprey calls, coyote howls, wind in canyons, lapping water, cliff swallow chatter. Silence at night (dark sky).
- **Flora**: Sagebrush flats (silvery-gray texture), pinyon-juniper (twisted trees, nut harvest), aspen (golden fall), ponderosa pines on rims (sparse, tall). Clear seasonal visual shifts.
- **Hazards animate well**: Afternoon wind, lightning storms, cold water, rattlesnake warnings.
- **Historical tie-in**: Powell 1869 expedition, dam 1962—minimal intrusion, suitable for "pocket vacation" tone.
- **DEM Data**: USGS 3DEP 1 m and 10 m available free; excellent for terrain generation.
- **2–4 km play area**: Red Canyon + Sheep Creek Bay offers geology, wildlife, water, and stunning red-rock backdrop without overcrowding.
