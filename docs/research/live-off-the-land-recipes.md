# Live-Off-the-Land Campfire Recipes

> **Reviewer corrections (2026-09-27, supersede the text below):**
> - **Sego lily** (*Calochortus nuttallii*) is a Great Basin plant: it belongs to **Flaming Gorge**, not Crater Lake. It is Utah's state flower, so the game should show it but not let the player dig it.
> - **Camas** does not grow on the caldera rim or Wizard Island (it grows in lower wet meadows such as Klamath Marsh), so it is lore only for Crater Lake. Its dangerous look-alike is **death camas** (*Toxicoscordion*), not bitterroot.
> - Pine-needle tea at Crater Lake should use **Shasta red fir / mountain hemlock** needles (both grow there); Douglas fir is scarce at the rim.

Research guide for Sojourney: two locations with authentic foraged & caught ingredients for relaxing "off-the-land" campfire cooking.

## Gatherable Ingredients by Location

### Crater Lake, Oregon (Cascades, ~1,900–2,200 m)

| Ingredient | Where Found | Season | How to Gather | Raw Edible? | Safety Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Huckleberries | Mountain slopes, forest floor | Aug–Sep | Pick by hand into container | Yes | None |
| Rainbow/Kokanee | Lake shore | Year-round (seasonal catch) | Fish with rod or nets | No | Cook thoroughly |
| Spring onions ("pani") | Moist meadows, streamside | Apr–May | Dig entire plant, whole or powdered | Yes | None |
| Camas | Wet meadows | Jun–Jul | Dig bulbs with soil knife | No (raw bitter) | Cook to turn to candy; avoid bitterroot confusion |
| Wild rose hips | Rose thickets, streamside | Fall–Winter | Pick firm, full-colored hips; use gloves | No | Remove hairy seeds; dry or steep for tea |
| Pine needle (Douglas fir, Noble fir) | Forest canopy | Year-round | Snip fresh needles | No | Avoid ponderosa; safe species only; not during pregnancy |
| Sago lilies | Forest floor, meadows | Summer | Dig bulbs carefully | No | Cook before eating; tuber-like |

### Flaming Gorge, Utah/Wyoming (~1,840 m, high desert)

| Ingredient | Where Found | Season | How to Gather | Raw Edible? | Safety Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pinyon pine nuts | Pinyon-juniper woodland | Fall (Oct–Nov) | Knock cones, bury overnight to open, crack shells | Yes (roasted best) | High labor; nutty flavor only after roasting |
| Lake trout / Kokanee / Rainbow | Lake shore | Year-round | Fish with rod; burbot in deep cold | No | Cook thoroughly |
| Serviceberry | Dry slopes, canyon edges | Jun–Jul | Pick ripe dark berries | Yes | Slightly tart; dry or cook into syrup |
| Chokecherry | Sagebrush edges, washes | Aug–Sep | Pick ripe dark berries | No (pits toxic) | **Strain all pits when cooking; never chew**. Cooking neutralizes cyanide; properly strained syrup is safe |
| Wild onion | Sagebrush flats, meadows | Spring | Dig bulbs, use whole or as powder | Yes | Mild onion flavor; no toxicity |
| Juniper berries | Juniper shrubs | Fall–Winter | Pick dark blue, waxy berries | No (bitter raw) | Use sparingly as spice; crush or grind; add to fish |
| Prickly pear cactus | Exposed dry slopes | Summer–Fall | Pick fruit (avoid spines), peel for pulp | Yes (pulp; peel first) | Dense spines; wear gloves; remove all glochids |
| Rose hips | Rose thickets | Fall–Winter | Pick and dry; remove seeds | No | Same as Crater Lake; high vitamin C |

## Recipes (Rucksack Staples: salt, pepper, oil/butter, flour, sugar, coffee/tea, foil, pan, pot)

### 1. **Foil-Packet Trout with Wild Onions**
**Location:** Crater Lake  
**Ingredients:** Fresh trout (gutted, ~30 cm), 2 wild onions (sliced), 2 tbsp butter, salt, pepper, foil  
**Method:** Lay trout on double-thickness foil, stuff cavity with onion, dot with butter, fold foil tight. Place on warm coals 10 min/side.  
**Time:** 20 min | **Flavor:** Delicate, onion-sweet, buttery

### 2. **Huckleberry Pancakes (Campfire)**
**Location:** Crater Lake  
**Ingredients:** 2 cups pancake mix, 1 cup water, 1 cup fresh huckleberries, 2 tbsp oil, salt  
**Method:** Mix dry ingredients ahead in ziplock. At camp, add water & berries, stir, pour onto oiled pan over coals.  
**Time:** 12 min | **Flavor:** Tart-sweet berry, fluffy golden breakfast

### 3. **Chokecherry Syrup (Strained)**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** 2 cups chokecherries (stemmed), 1 cup sugar, 1 tbsp water, cheesecloth or jelly bag  
**Method:** Simmer berries + water in pot 10 min until bursting. Pour through cheesecloth *without pressing* to strain all pits. Return liquid to pot, add sugar, simmer 5 min to thicken.  
**Time:** 20 min | **Flavor:** Deep purple, tart-sweet, safe when strained | **Safety:** Never press the bag; discard all pulp

### 4. **Pine-Needle Tea (Douglas Fir)**
**Location:** Crater Lake  
**Ingredients:** Handful fresh Douglas fir needles, 2 cups water, sugar or honey (optional)  
**Method:** Bring water to boil in pot, drop in needles, simmer 5 min. Strain. Serve hot or cold.  
**Time:** 8 min | **Flavor:** Citrus-piney, warming, vitamin C boost | **Safety Note:** Only use Douglas/Noble fir; avoid ponderosa; not for pregnant players

### 5. **Juniper-Seasoned Lake Trout**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** Lake trout (gutted), 12 juniper berries (crushed), 2 tbsp butter, salt, foil  
**Method:** Crush berries with rock; coat inside cavity. Butter foil, lay trout, sprinkle exterior with berries & salt. Fold foil, cook on coals 10 min/side.  
**Time:** 22 min | **Flavor:** Piney-gin notes, elegant, lean fish

### 6. **Prickly Pear Lemonade**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** 4 prickly pear fruits (peeled, diced), 2 tbsp sugar, 2 tbsp foraged wild onion juice or lemon (if available), 2 cups water, salt  
**Method:** Simmer diced pulp + water 5 min. Strain to remove seeds. Add sugar, stir. Serve cold or warm.  
**Time:** 10 min | **Flavor:** Watermelon-pink, sweet-tart, thirst-quenching

### 7. **Roasted Pinyon Nuts (Campfire)**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** Pinyon nuts in shell, salt  
**Method:** Gather cones, bury overnight to split. In closed pan or foil packet over low coals, stir frequently 10–15 min until fragrant. Crack shells at table.  
**Time:** 15 min (+ overnight cone-opening) | **Flavor:** Buttery, delicate, nutty | **Note:** High-effort but culturally significant Ute harvest

### 8. **Serviceberry-Rose Hip Syrup (Forage Blend)**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** 1 cup serviceberries, ½ cup rose hips (dried or fresh), 1 cup sugar, 2 tbsp water  
**Method:** Simmer berries + hips 10 min until soft. Strain (rose hip seeds are itchy; use jelly bag). Return juice, add sugar, simmer 5 min.  
**Time:** 18 min | **Flavor:** Autumn spice, tart-rich, medicinal warmth

### 9. **Burbot "Poor Man's Lobster" (Boiled with Salt)**
**Location:** Flaming Gorge  
**Ingredients:** Fresh burbot (skinned, chunked), 2 cups water, 1 tbsp salt, 2 tbsp butter (if available)  
**Method:** Bring salted water to boil in pot. Gently simmer burbot chunks 3 min (do not boil hard). Serve with butter.  
**Time:** 8 min | **Flavor:** Firm, sweet, cod-like, briny tender | **Note:** Competes with kokanee; ethical catch

### 10. **Camas "Candy" (Crater Lake Staple)**
**Location:** Crater Lake  
**Ingredients:** Camas bulbs (cleaned), pit or fire, water, cloth wrapping  
**Method:** Wrap cleaned bulbs in foil or cloth, bury in coals 4–6 hours (traditional pit method shown). OR boil in water 1 hour until soft. Let cool, peel (optional), eat sweet.  
**Time:** 60 min (baked) / 65 min (boiled) | **Flavor:** Starchy, sweet (if well-cooked; raw is bitter) | **Cultural note:** Klamath staple

### 11. **Wild Rose Hip Tea (High Vitamin C)**
**Location:** Either location  
**Ingredients:** ¼ cup fresh rose hips or 1 tbsp dried (crushed), 1 cup water, honey (optional)  
**Method:** Bring water to boil, drop in hips, steep covered 15 min. Strain carefully (remove all hairy seeds).  
**Time:** 20 min | **Flavor:** Tart, floral, energizing | **Health:** 60x more vitamin C than citrus

### 12. **Rainbow Trout on a Stick (Minimalist)**
**Location:** Crater Lake  
**Ingredients:** Fresh gutted trout (~30 cm), green stick (willow or alder), salt, fire  
**Method:** Split a green stick lengthwise, pin trout between halves (or skewer), hold 15 cm above coals, rotating every 3 min.  
**Time:** 12 min | **Flavor:** Smoky, flaky, primal campfire | **Skill:** Requires fire sense; no foil/pan

## Game Design Notes

### Recipe Difficulty & Progression

**Easy / Ambient Mode** (no failure, focus on immersion):
- Huckleberry pancakes, foil-packet trout, syrups (minimal prep, rewarding visually)
- Pine-needle tea, rose hip tea (gather → steep → sip, relax mechanic)
- Roasted pinyon nuts (labor-light campfire scene; Ute cultural moment)
- Rainbow trout on a stick (iconic, beginner-friendly fire skill)

**Hard / Survival-Lite Mode** (timing, resource scarcity, some failure):
- Camas candy (requires foraging accuracy, long cook time, reward is subtle)
- Chokecherry syrup (risk: if player doesn't strain pits, toxicity mechanic teaches caution)
- Juniper-trout (more technical catch + seasoning; demands fresh herbs)
- Burbot boil (ethical tension: "trophy fish" vs. lake ecosystem; conditional availability)

### Gameplay Effects (Gentle, Optional Warmth/Energy)

- **Huckleberry pancakes**: +10 energy, cosmetic "full belly" animation
- **Trout (any method)**: +15 energy, campfire satisfaction tone
- **Syrups / jams**: Edible decoration; +5 energy if consumed fresh
- **Teas (pine-needle, rose hip)**: +5 warmth if cold, psychological "well-being" HUD icon
- **Pinyon nuts**: +8 energy, cultural achievement badge (Ute harvest commemoration)
- **Camas**: +12 energy (slow food; delayed gratification reward)
- **Burbot**: +18 energy (prestige catch; moral choice moment if eco-messaging on)

### Foraging Time Investment

- **Quick picks** (berries, onions, rose hips): 2–5 min in-game
- **Medium effort** (pinyon cones, wild onions, serviceberries): 10–20 min, scattered across terrain
- **High effort** (camas bulb dig, specific fire setup): 20–40 min, teaches patience

### Cultural Authenticity & Educational Layer

- **Klamath/Modoc practices** (Crater Lake): Emphasize huckleberry gathering as social/seasonal ritual; camas as survival staple
- **Ute/Shoshone practices** (Flaming Gorge): Pinyon harvest as ceremonial autumn event; juniper + wild onion as high-desert adaptation
- Optional "Lore" popups: Traditional harvest blessings, Indigenous food security context (avoiding stereotypes)

### Safety Teaching Moments

- **Chokecherry syrup recipe**: Interactive straining mechanic; failure → visual "pit hazard" warning (no actual harm, just learning)
- **Pine-needle tea caution**: Hover-text notes ponderosa danger; pregnancy warning (for players who want lore depth)
- **Rose hips**: Glove-wearing animation (respect for thorns, practical detail)
